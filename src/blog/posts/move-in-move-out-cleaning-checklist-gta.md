Losing a rental deposit over a dirty oven or a dusty baseboard is one of the most avoidable expenses in the entire moving process.

Between packing boxes, coordinating elevator bookings in a downtown Toronto condo, and juggling closing dates in Mississauga or Vaughan, cleaning is usually the task that gets rushed or skipped entirely. But whether you're a tenant trying to get your full deposit back, a landlord prepping a unit for new tenants, or a homeowner buying a resale property, a proper move-in/move-out clean is what separates a smooth transition from a stressful one.

## Why Move-In/Move-Out Cleaning Is Different From Regular Cleaning

A standard weekly or biweekly clean maintains a home that's already reasonably tidy. Move-in/move-out cleaning is a different job entirely — it's a deep, detailed reset of a space that's either been lived in for years or sat vacant, often with dust, grease buildup, and grime in spots that never get touched during routine cleaning.

Property managers and landlords across the GTA increasingly expect (or require in the lease) that a unit be returned in "broom clean" or better condition. On the flip side, if you're moving into a new place, you don't know how the previous occupants treated it, so a full clean before you unpack gives you a genuinely fresh, sanitized start.

## Room-by-Room Checklist

Use this as a working checklist whether you're cleaning yourself or briefing a cleaning company on what "done" looks like.

### Kitchen

- Degrease and wipe down the inside and outside of the oven, including racks
- Clean the stovetop, range hood, and exhaust filter
- Wipe inside and outside of the refrigerator (unplug and prop the door open if it's vacant)
- Empty, wipe, and disinfect all cabinets and drawers, inside and out
- Clean the sink, faucet, and backsplash
- Degrease and wipe down countertops
- Clean the exterior of the dishwasher and microwave, inside and out

### Bathrooms

- Scrub and disinfect the toilet, tub, and shower, including grout lines
- Clean the showerhead and remove any mineral buildup
- Wipe down the vanity, mirror, and cabinets inside and out
- Clean the exhaust fan cover
- Mop the floor and clean behind the toilet base

### Bedrooms and Living Areas

- Dust and wipe down all baseboards, window sills, and door frames
- Clean inside closets, including shelves and rods
- Spot-clean walls and switch plates
- Vacuum carpets thoroughly, including edges and corners
- Wash or mop hard floors
- Clean interior windows and window tracks

### Entryway and Extras

- Wipe down light fixtures and ceiling fans
- Clean vents and radiator covers
- Wipe balcony or patio doors and tracks
- If it's a house rather than a condo, give the entryway a once-over for salt and grit tracked in from winter — even in warmer months, driveway and porch grime builds up fast in the GTA

## Timing It Right

For renters, plan the deep clean for after your furniture is out but before your final walkthrough with the landlord or property manager — an empty unit is far easier to clean thoroughly since you can reach every wall and corner. For buyers, a pre-move-in clean should happen after the previous owners have vacated but before your own furniture arrives, ideally the same week as closing so the home stays fresh when you move in.

If you're managing a rental turnover between tenants in a busy building, tight timelines are common. Many GTA condo buildings only allow moving trucks and elevator bookings during specific windows, so cleaning often has to happen in a narrow gap between the outgoing and incoming tenant.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Forgetting the inside of appliances, which is usually the first thing a landlord or new buyer checks
- Skipping window tracks and blinds, which collect a surprising amount of dust
- Using harsh abrasive cleaners on granite, quartz, or natural stone countertops — stick to pH-neutral cleaners to avoid dulling or etching the surface
- Not checking closets and cabinet interiors before declaring the job done
- Leaving the balcony, patio, or garage for "later" and then running out of time
