An office that only gets cleaned when it "looks dirty" is already behind, because most of what makes a workspace unhealthy is invisible until someone's sick.

Deciding how often to clean a commercial space isn't as simple as picking nightly, weekly, or monthly off a menu. The right frequency depends on foot traffic, the nature of the business, the number of employees sharing the space, and how visible the office is to clients. Get it wrong in either direction and you either waste money on unnecessary visits or let grime and germs build up to the point where it affects staff health and client impressions.

## Why Office Cleaning Frequency Matters

A clean office isn't just about appearances, though first impressions matter plenty when clients walk into a Bay Street office or a boardroom in Mississauga. Shared spaces — desks, doorknobs, kitchen counters, washrooms — are where germs spread fastest in any workplace. Regular cleaning reduces the spread of common colds and flu, especially during the fall and winter months when GTA offices tend to see more sick days and more grit and salt tracked in from outside.

There's also a productivity angle. Employees notice when common areas are neglected, and a consistently clean space signals that the business takes its environment, and by extension its people, seriously.

## How Often Should You Clean, by Office Type

### Daily Cleaning

High-traffic offices, client-facing spaces, medical or dental offices, and any workplace with more than a handful of staff generally need daily attention for:

- Washrooms (restocking supplies, disinfecting fixtures)
- Kitchen and breakroom counters
- Trash and recycling removal
- High-touch surfaces like door handles, light switches, and elevator buttons
- Entryway floors, especially during wet or salty winter months

### 2–3 Times Per Week

Smaller offices or those with lower foot traffic can often get away with cleaning a few times a week rather than daily, covering:

- Vacuuming and floor care throughout the office
- Desk and workstation surface wipe-downs (excluding personal items)
- Washroom deep sanitizing
- Kitchen appliance cleaning, including microwaves and fridges

### Weekly Cleaning

Very small offices, co-working spaces with light usage, or satellite offices with only a few people on-site occasionally may only need a weekly visit for general tidying, dusting, and floor care.

### Monthly or Quarterly Deep Cleans

Regardless of how often routine cleaning happens, most offices benefit from a deeper clean on a longer cycle to handle things day-to-day cleaning doesn't reach:

- Carpet shampooing or deep extraction cleaning
- Window washing, interior and exterior where accessible
- Vent and air duct dusting
- Upholstery cleaning on office chairs and reception furniture
- Behind and under large furniture and equipment

## A Quick Self-Assessment Checklist

Answer these to get a sense of where your office falls on the frequency spectrum:

1. Do more than 10 people use the space daily?
2. Do clients or visitors regularly come into the office?
3. Is there a shared kitchen, breakroom, or washroom?
4. Is the business in an industry with higher hygiene expectations (medical, food service, childcare)?
5. Does the office see heavy foot traffic from outside, particularly through GTA winters when salt, slush, and road grime get tracked in?

If you answered yes to three or more, daily or near-daily cleaning is likely worth the investment. If you answered yes to one or none, two to three times a week or even weekly service may be enough.

## Industry-Specific Considerations

Medical and dental offices, daycares, and food-service businesses generally need daily disinfecting regardless of size, due to higher hygiene standards and regulatory expectations. Professional services like law firms, accounting offices, and tech companies have more flexibility, since the main concerns are shared surfaces and general tidiness rather than clinical-level sanitation. Retail and hospitality businesses fall somewhere in between, needing frequent attention to public-facing areas even if back-office spaces can go longer between cleans.

## Adjusting for the Seasons

Cleaning frequency isn't static throughout the year. Flu season typically calls for more frequent disinfecting of high-touch surfaces, and winter months bring extra floor maintenance needs as employees and visitors track in salt, slush, and moisture from outside. Ramping up entryway and floor cleaning between November and March is a small adjustment that keeps floors from getting damaged and reduces slip hazards.
